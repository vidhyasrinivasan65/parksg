## 2. My findings

### Finding 1 — A carpark with free lots is shown as FULL
- Where: https://parksg-seven.vercel.app, the Orchard zone, the BLK 14A FARRER PARK ROAD card.
- What I did, what I saw: I opened the Orchard zone and read down the list. The Farrer Park card carries a red FULL badge, while the banner inside the same card reads that the carpark has 310 lots. The same card also gives two different distances, 2.4 km at the top and 100 m in the banner. Reading the Orchard route directly at /api/carparks?zone=Orchard shows why: BLK 14A FARRER PARK ROAD is returned twice, once as KJM2-C with 314 lots and once as KJML-C with 0 lots. The same happens to BLK 668 CHANDER ROAD, returned as BKE9-C with 28 lots and BKE1-C with 0.
- Which heuristic: 1, Visibility of System Status.
- Screen or system: System. The route sends two separate records that share one carpark name, so the screen has no way to know they are the same place, and takes the badge from one record and the lot count from the other. The screen cannot repair this with what it is sent.
- Severity, and why: 4, driven by whether it damages the product's standing out of proportion. A driver who is told a carpark is full when 314 lots are free has been sent away from the place they wanted, and after that they have no reason to believe any other number on the page. Nielsen's 4 is imperative to fix before release, and a product whose central number can be flatly wrong should not be released.
- The repair: A carpark appears once in the list, with one lot count and one distance, and the badge on the card is calculated from the same number the card displays.

### Finding 2 — A zone that does not exist returns a full list of carparks
- Where: https://parksg-seven.vercel.app/api/carparks?zone=Banana
- What I did, what I saw: I asked the route for a zone that does not exist. Instead of refusing, it returned 92 carparks, labelled "zone": "Orchard", with a centre point of 1.3048, 103.8318. Nothing in the response says the zone I asked for was not recognised, so a wrong request came back looking like a correct answer.
- Which heuristic: 9, Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors.
- Screen or system: System. The route already knows which four zones it supports, so it is the route that should reject an unknown one. The screen cannot tell the difference, because what comes back is a valid, complete, correctly labelled list.
- Severity, and why: 4, driven by whether it damages the product's standing out of proportion. A request that should have failed produces confident, real-looking data, and anything built on this route, including a future search box or a shared link with a zone in it, would silently show the wrong zone's carparks.
- The repair: A zone the product does not support is refused, with a response that says the zone was not recognised and names the zones that are available.

### Finding 3 — Distances are written in two different units on the same screen
- Where: https://parksg-seven.vercel.app, the Orchard zone, down the list of cards.
- What I did, what I saw: I read the distances down the Orchard list. Orchard Central, at 997 metres, is shown as "1000 m", and Orchard Point, at 1052 metres, is shown as "1.1 km". The two carparks are about fifty metres apart, but their distances are written in different units, so I could not compare them at a glance.
- Which heuristic: 4, Consistency and Standards.
- Screen or system: Screen. The route already sends the distance in metres for every carpark, so the screen has everything it needs and only the way it writes the number is wrong.
- Severity, and why: 2, driven by whether the person can learn around it. A driver works out after a moment that 1000 m and 1.1 km are the same kind of distance, so it slows the comparison rather than stopping it.
- The repair: Every distance on the screen is written in the same unit, so that two carparks can be compared without converting one of them.

### Finding 4 — The navigation control is the smallest thing on the card
- Where: https://parksg-seven.vercel.app, any zone, on each carpark card, beneath the location name.
- What I did, what I saw: On both my phone and my laptop I picked a carpark and looked for the way to navigate to it. The navigation control sits under the location name in small text. I was able to tap it, but it is the smallest element on a card whose whole purpose is to send a driver to that carpark, so it does not look like the main thing to press.
- Which heuristic: 7, Flexibility and Efficiency of Use.
- Screen or system: Screen. The control is already on the card and already works, so only its size and prominence are wrong.
- Severity, and why: 2, driven by how often it happens. Every driver who chooses a carpark needs this control, but nothing fails when they find it, so it costs them a moment rather than the task.
- The repair: The navigation control is the most prominent control on the card, and is large enough to be an obvious tap target on a phone.
